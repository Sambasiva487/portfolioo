Accenture × JPMC ,
banking interfaces that feel effortless

Two years on the JPMC engagement via Accenture: secure.chase.com-style auth flows, account dashboards, and dense internal workflow tools. Trust, accessibility, and speed were the deliverables, not extra polish.

Role
UI/UX Designer
Timeline
Oct 2021, Aug 2023
Stack
Figma · Prototypes · WCAG
Scope
Enterprise financial platforms

The problem

Enterprise financial tools are typically bolted together over years: screens built by different teams, data tables that don't match, workflows that require training manuals to complete. Users (analysts, ops, compliance) were spending time fighting the interface instead of doing analysis. And accessibility was inconsistent, some flows met WCAG, others didn't.

My role

  • Owned UI/UX deliverables across several MyJPMC / internal workflow pages in an agile setting.
  • Delivered wireframes, interactive prototypes, and final responsive designs for data-heavy tables, dashboards, and workflows.
  • Partnered with devs on implementation and with accessibility reviewers for WCAG AA compliance.

Key decisions

1. Progressive disclosure, not removal

We didn't strip features (the tools had to stay complete), we collapsed depth by default: common tasks visible, rare actions reveal on demand. The auth step is the only mandatory gate, after that, everything defers to user context.

2. Auth is the first impression

The sign-in screen is technically a log-in form. Functionally, it's the bank's handshake. Clean, predictable, instantly familiar.

3. Tables serve the decision

Every table was designed to answer a question, not just display data. Columns colored, sorted, and nested by decision relevance.

Constraint ≠ limitation. Enterprise design forces you to solve under strict rules, that's where genuinely better design shows up.
